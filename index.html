Beginner guide: your first 3D engraving on a fiber laser

Written for galvo fiber lasers running LightBurn (for example a ComMarker B6 MOPA). Open each step in order.

1How depth engraving works, in one minute

A depth map is a black-and-white picture where brightness means height. LightBurn's 3D Sliced mode burns the picture many times in thin layers. Each pass removes a little metal, and darker pixels get burned in more passes, so they end up deeper.

  • White = not engraved (the top surface of your design)
  • Black = engraved the deepest
  • Grays = everything in between

This page makes that picture from a 3D model (STL file). The top of the model becomes white, so it stays as the polished surface and everything around it gets carved down.

2Get a 3D model

Fastest start: press Try sample above. It's a 40 mm star medallion made for a first test.

Free models: search Printables or Thingiverse for bas relief, coin, medallion, relief or emblem. Flat-backed reliefs work best. A full 3D figure also works, but only the side you choose (Top, Front or Side) gets carved.

Make your own: Tinkercad (free, in the browser) is enough. Build it in millimeters and export STL.

Size matters. This page reads STL numbers as millimeters, so a model 40 wide comes out 40 mm wide in LightBurn. If yours comes out tiny or huge, it was modeled in other units. Rescale it in LightBurn with the lock on so it doesn't stretch.
3Make the depth map on this page
  1. Drop the STL in the box above, or press Try sample.
  2. View: Top for most reliefs and coins. If your model looks wrong, try Front or Side.
  3. Bit depth: 16-bit (already selected). LightBurn 2.1 or newer reads it, and it keeps the depth smooth when you run hundreds of passes.
  4. Resolution: about 40 pixels per mm of part is a good match for fine laser settings. A 40 mm part needs about 1600, and a 25 mm part about 1000. Bigger isn't better past that, it only slows LightBurn down.
  5. Background: Carve away sinks everything around the model so it stands up in relief, like a coin. Leave keeps the surrounding metal and engraves only the model's shape, which is faster.
  6. Invert: leave it off.
  7. Press Download and save the PNG.
Check the preview: the high parts of your model should be the brightest. If the design looks like a photo negative, the model is upside down. Try another View, or turn on Invert.
4Set it up in LightBurn
  1. File > Import (or drag the PNG onto the work area). It comes in at the model's real size.
  2. Move it to where your part sits. Use the red-light preview (Frame) to check the position on the actual metal.
  3. Double-click the layer to open Cut Settings. Set Mode to Image, then set Image Mode to 3D Sliced.
  4. Set Number of Passes. More passes cut deeper and smoother but take longer. See the starting numbers in step 5.
  5. Turn on Enable cleanup pass. It runs a light pass every so often (Clean after # of passes) to clear the burnt crust, which keeps the depth even.
  6. Open Preview and turn on Show traversal moves. The red areas should grow larger with each pass. If they do, the depth map is the right way around.
3D Sliced shows up only for galvo lasers like fiber machines. If you can't find it, check that LightBurn knows your machine is a galvo, and that you have LightBurn 2.1 or newer.
Have LightBurn 2.2 or newer? It can import an STL directly (File > Import the .stl) and build the depth map itself. This page is still handy: it works with older versions, previews quickly in the browser, and gives you a PNG you can reuse and share.
5Starting settings for a 60W MOPA (test first)

ComMarker publishes a LightBurn 3D Slice example for its B6 60W MOPA on a brass coin. Use these numbers as a starting point, not as gospel:

Power90%30%
Speed2000 mm/s6000 mm/s
Frequency100 kHz75 kHz
Number of Passes512 
Line Interval0.025 mm 
Hatch angle45° (in LightBurn: Angle Increment) 

Source: ComMarker B6 MOPA 3D relief examples. Different language versions of that page list slightly different cleanup timing, which is another reason to test.

  • Do a small test first. Scale the design down to about 15 mm and drop to around 100 passes. A few minutes on scrap tells you more than an hour of guessing.
  • Too shallow? Add passes. That's the main depth control.
  • Rough or burnt? Turn the cleanup pass on, or run it more often.
  • Steps or terraces in curves? Use 16-bit and more passes.
  • LightBurn's Material Test (under Laser Tools) can burn a grid where each square uses a different power or speed. It's the quickest way to dial in a new metal.
Depth jobs with hundreds of passes can run 30 minutes to a few hours, and the metal gets hot. Never leave the laser running unattended.
6Safety basics for fiber lasers
  • Eyes: the beam is invisible infrared (about 1064 nm). Always wear the goggles rated for that wavelength, including when you're just watching. Regular sunglasses do nothing.
  • Fumes: run a fume extractor or vent outdoors. Metal vapor and coatings are bad to breathe.
  • Materials: never laser PVC or vinyl (they release chlorine gas). If you don't know what a plastic is, don't laser it.
  • Heat: deep jobs make the part hot. Let it cool, or use pliers.
  • Fire: keep the area clear, and keep an extinguisher nearby.
